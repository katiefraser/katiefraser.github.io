01

Safety impact of fine-tuning large vision-language models

Previous research has shown that when text-only LLMs are fine-tuned, even with benign data, it can lead to an unintended degradation of the safety guardrails. However, the impact of fine-tuning on multimodal (vision-language) models is less-studied. This project will investigate how parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods affect model safety by comparing safety behavior before and after fine-tuning. The project involves running large-scale models on GPU clusters, fine-tuning open-source multimodal models on benign datasets, and evaluating capability and safety using established multimodal safety benchmarks.

02

Automated detection of anti-Black racism in online texts

This project is a partnership with the Decoding Hate project at NYU, who have developed a detailed, theory-driven taxonomy of anti-Black racism to be used by (human) data annotators. This project will aim to translate the taxonomy into an automated tool to help human annotators in their work. This will involve assessing multiple NLP methods for fine-grained hate speech classification, evaluating their accuracy, and determining which parts of the process can be automated and which must be performed by humans.

The ideal candidate for this project will have strong technical/programming skills as well as background or interest in sociolinguistics and social justice.
